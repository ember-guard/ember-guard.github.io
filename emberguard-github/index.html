One perimeter, two directions

Wildfire and battery fire are the same problem seen from opposite sides of the fence. Ember Guard handles both with one system.

Satellite fire data and weather forecasts tell us when a fire could reach the site. Before it arrives, the perimeter wets the buffer and the surfaces embers land on, so embers die at the fence line.

  1. Fire forecast to reach the site
  2. Perimeter wets the buffer ahead of it
  3. Embers stopped at the fence line

If a battery unit catches fire, exposure cooling and the treated buffer stop it spreading to the units beside it or the dry land beyond the fence, while the fire service gets a live feed.

  1. A battery unit catches fire
  2. Fire service linked live
  3. Cooling holds it inside the fence

From forecast to fence line

Ember Guard acts on what a fire is about to do, so protection is in place before the first ember lands.

  1. 1

    Watch

    We track active fires and fire weather around every site, day and night, using satellite fire data and forecasts.

  2. 2

    Forecast

    For each site we estimate when a fire could arrive, so the system acts on a forecast instead of waiting for smoke at the gate.

  3. 3

    Protect

  4. 4

Fire now decides which energy projects get built

Counties, fire authorities and insurers are asking every new battery site the same question: what happens when fire comes?

  • At least 96 US battery projects, about 25 GW in total, have faced public pushback.

    Source: Energy Tech

  • Orange County now bans battery sites in Very High fire hazard zones, and San Benito County has adopted a moratorium of up to 10 months on large projects.

    Source: EticaAGSource: Citizen Portal

  • The risk runs both ways. A wildfire can burn into a site, and a battery fire can burn out of one, as at Moss Landing in January 2025.

    Source: CalMatters

  • California insurers must now reward wildfire mitigation on commercial property.

    Source: California Department of Insurance
